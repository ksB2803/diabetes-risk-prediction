How it works

Five steps, in this order. The test set is put aside before any tuning, so its scores show how a model does on data it never learned from.

  1. Upload a CSV

    Check the preview, column types and missing values.

  2. Pick the answer column

    Choose the yes/no target and the input features.

  3. Split and tune

    Hold out a test set, then tune with cross-validation on training data only.

  4. Compare models

    See cross-validation and test scores side by side.

  5. Try a prediction

    Enter values for one record and see the model’s output.

Did you know?

General background from public health agencies. It is not personal medical advice.

It is about blood glucose

Diabetes is a long-term condition in which blood glucose stays too high, because the body makes too little insulin or cannot use it well.

Source: World Health Organization

Type 1 and type 2 differ

In type 1 the body makes little or no insulin. In type 2, the most common form, the body does not use insulin effectively.

Source: World Health Organization

Type 2 can be quiet at first

Type 2 symptoms often develop over several years, and some people do not notice any. Knowing risk factors and getting checked matters.

Source: US Centers for Disease Control and Prevention

It can affect many organs

Over time, high blood glucose can damage blood vessels and nerves, affecting the heart, eyes, kidneys and feet.

Source: World Health Organization

Activity helps

Regular physical activity helps prevent type 2 diabetes and is part of healthy day-to-day management.

Source: World Health Organization

Diagnosis needs clinical tests

Doctors diagnose diabetes with blood tests such as A1C or fasting plasma glucose, usually confirmed by a second result. A model demo like this one cannot diagnose anyone.

Source: US National Institute of Diabetes and Digestive and Kidney Diseases
